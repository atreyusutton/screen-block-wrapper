# AI-Powered "Intentional Use" Gates for Social Media on iPhone (research current to 20 Oct 2026)

Scope: products and research on friction / intention prompts before opening distracting apps; how an AI agent could judge a stated reason ("cooking instructions", "car-building videos"), decide whether and how long to unlock, and run a later "checkup", built on iPhone. Dates are given wherever the source has one. Reddit (r/nosurf, r/digitalminimalism) could not be searched: the search tool blocks reddit.com, so user voice comes from App Store reviews, vendor forums, press reviews and study surveys instead.

---

## 1. Existing products using friction / intention prompts or AI gatekeepers. How they work, what users say

### Takeaway
Intention prompts are already a standard feature (one sec, ScreenZen, Clearspace, Jomo; Opal users are asking for it), but they are almost all self-report text boxes or pick-lists with no judging. By Oct 2026 a few "AI gatekeeper" apps exist: Binky Block (negotiate with a chimp coach), Overlord/Forfeit (chat accountability partner that checks evidence), and Accountable AI (AI checks proof). None has independent reviews or published efficacy data, and I found no well-covered app where an LLM reads your stated reason and sets the unlock minutes. That niche looks open, but nobody has shown it works.

### Cited Findings
**Friction / intention apps (non-AI)**
- **one sec (core mechanic):** catches attempts to open chosen apps and shows a full-screen breathing pop-up. It includes the number of opening attempts in the past 24 h, a ~10 s delay with a moving-screen effect, a short deliberation message, and an option to dismiss. On iOS it is triggered by automations the user sets up in the Shortcuts app (as described in the 2023 PNAS paper). — [Grüning et al., PNAS 2023 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9974409)
- **one sec "Intention Tracking" (iPhone/iPad only):** an extra reflection step after the breathing exercise asks why you are opening the app. You pick from a preset list or type a custom intention. If the "app-closing reminder" is also on, one sec sends a notification after you open the target app "to remind you about your intention… and to check if you're still doing what you initially planned to do." — [one sec help center: Intention Tracking](https://tutorials.one-sec.app/en/articles/3035138); [feedback board "reminder of intent" (marked completed)](https://feedback.one-sec.app/p/reminder-of-intent)
- one sec pricing is reported as free for one app, then $19.99/yr. This comes from a 2026 third-party review that I could not cross-check. — [learnofchrist.com review](https://learnofchrist.com/resources/one-sec)
- **ScreenZen:** lets the app start loading, then shows a pause screen with a customizable countdown and/or a prompt such as "what are you seeking?". Each app can have its own pause length, from a few seconds to a few minutes. — [Habitdoom (competitor blog)](https://habitdoom.com/blog/screenzen-alternative-iphone); [Nibble (competitor blog)](https://nibble-app.com/blog/screenzen); [Aberystwyth Univ. student blog, Feb 2024](https://wordpress.aber.ac.uk/digital-capabilities/2024/02/)
- ScreenZen's own site: limited unlocks per day, scheduled blocks, a "lock mode that protects against common loopholes," an option to prevent uninstalling, and "completely free" (donation-supported). Self-reported stats: 4.8 stars from 30K+ ratings, 500,000+ monthly users, "over a million" joined, users "take back about 3 hours" a day (self-reported). One reviewer quoted on the site says changing the phone's clock can bypass time blocks. — [screenzen.co](https://www.screenzen.co/)
- How-To Geek reviewer: a short ScreenZen timer becomes routine within days, so lengthen the pause for problem apps. — [How-To Geek via Scour](https://scour.ing/p/https://www.howtogeek.com/download-this-screen-time-app-your-eyes-will-thank-you-later)
- WhistleOut reviewer (one person, about a month of testing on Instagram, TikTok and YouTube): daily use fell to ~30 min/day within two weeks, and the streak counter helped. Anecdotal. — [WhistleOut ScreenZen review](https://www.whistleout.ca/CellPhones/Guides/screenzen-app-review)
- **Clearspace:** before a restricted app opens, it asks "why are you doing that," then shows a quote with options to open the app or exit, "prompting you to check in with your intentions." Onboarding asks why you want to cut back, not only what. — [Union Street Journal (student paper), Dec 2025](https://unionstreetjournal.com/2025/12/the-clearspace-way/)
- **Opal:** "Deep Focus" is the "maximum level of protection — you can't bypass or cancel." Blocking runs on Apple's Screen Time API. Pricing is $19.99/mo or $99/yr for unlimited recurring sessions and harder blocking. — [Opal App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/il/app/id1497465230); [TechCrunch, 25 Dec 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/25/the-best-distraction-blockers-to-jumpstart-your-focus-in-the-new-year)
- Opal community users have asked for intention-setting to be *required* when a limit is reached, skipped or snoozed. This is a user request, not a confirmed feature. — [Opal community forum](https://community.opal.so/t/set-an-intention-before-opening-an-app/436)
- **Jomo:** "Breaks" make you prove you want the app: wait, type, enter a password, or state an intention. A TapSmart review describes a cool-off timer plus agreeing to a Break of up to 15 minutes. An App Store reviewer admits: "sometimes I'll be bad and just type in gibberish or a single letter to bypass it" and asked for a strict version. "Strict Mode makes your rules unbreakable." Plus tier: $5.99/mo, $29.99/yr, $99.99 lifetime. — [jomo.so/features](https://jomo.so/features); [TapSmart review](https://www.tapsmart.com/apps/review-jomo/?amp=1); [Jomo App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/lc/app/jomo-screen-time-blocker/id1609960918)
- **Freedom:** blocks across devices (a session started on a laptop shows a blocked screen on the iPhone). "Locked Mode" prevents ending a session early. From $3.33/mo billed annually, $8.99 monthly, or $199 lifetime. — [TechCrunch, 25 Dec 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/25/the-best-distraction-blockers-to-jumpstart-your-focus-in-the-new-year)
- **Brick (physical NFC token):** tap the phone on the device to lock apps, tap again to unlock. Locking can be started remotely, but unlocking always needs the physical tap. The app gives 5 lifetime emergency unlocks per device, then you must contact support. Without "Strict Mode" you can just delete the app. Reviews are mixed: one reviewer remembered where the Brick was whenever the itch came, another felt "the familiar itch returned" within a day. Price is reported as $59 in one source and $92 plus shipping in another. — [Trail & Kale review](https://trailandkale.com/brick-app-blocker-review/); [Man of Many review](https://manofmany.com/tech/brick-app-blocker-review-analysis); [WhistleOut Brick review](https://www.whistleout.com/CellPhones/Apps/brick-for-phone-limits-app-review)

**AI-based gatekeepers (2025–2026)**
- **Binky Block (iPhone, "New on iPhone", ©2026):** when you open a blocked app, a baby-chimp coach appears instead. You "make your case" and he may grant a short unlock (examples: "5 minutes", "a few mins back") or deny it. Five coaching styles; a "Touch Grass" mode suggests alternatives. It is powered by "a conversational system tuned to his personality and your chosen coaching style," but the site never says whether that is an LLM. It is built on Screen Time / Family Controls and receives only opaque tokens, so it cannot see which apps you blocked. The site describes no anti-lying measures. Data collected: account email, anonymous analytics, opaque tokens and subscription state, stored encrypted with Supabase. Free tier covers one app; paid tier is $6.99/wk, $14.99/mo or $59.99/yr. The testimonials look founder-written. — [binkyblock.app](https://binkyblock.app/)
- **Overlord (inside the Forfeit app, iOS/Android, plus Mac monitor; also via iMessage, Telegram, WhatsApp):** a chat-based AI accountability partner. Chosen apps are blocked by default and Overlord decides temporary unblocks from your rules plus live signals: calendar, GPS, Apple Health heart rate, and verified photos/videos. Example: it denied a request during a focus block, then unblocked Reddit when the calendar showed a free window. Unblock lengths are set per rule (examples range from 1 to 30 min, ~10 min per earned Instagram session). Anti-lying: cross-checks claims against integrations, and checks photo proof for freshness, EXIF timestamps and GPS, flagging camera-roll or staged images. Screen Time passwords have a 24-hour cooldown. Money stakes are possible (e.g., $5 per extra minute), held via Stripe; bank charges are scanned through Plaid. The site does not name the LLM. — [overlord.app](https://overlord.app/)
- **Accountable AI – Habit Blocker (v1.0 released 16 Dec 2025):** you set a goal with a deadline; if you miss it, chosen apps are blocked through the Screen Time API until you submit photo or text proof. The AI checks the proof's metadata, timestamps and relevance. Rated 4.9 from only 11 ratings; age rating 13+. A reviewer says the AI "figured out when I was trying to fool it to get my TikTok access back." The privacy label lists photos and user content as linked to identity, and proof photos are deleted after 30 days. — [Accountable AI App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/accountable-ai-habit-blocker/id6747360923)
- **Physical-task unlocks with on-device AI:** PushUp Time counts reps with on-device AI and grants 15–120 min of timed access. FlowLocker tracks squats and pushups with the camera. Walk to Unlock uses Apple Health step goals. — [PushUp Time App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6749405784); [FlowLocker App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ai/app/flowlocker-app-blocker/id6754835510); [Walk to Unlock App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/walk-to-unlock-app-blocker/id6743841470)
- A general search for apps where you "explain to an AI" to unlock found no product of that exact shape (a typed justification judged by a chatbot that sets unlock time) other than Binky Block. — [search result set incl. shiftyourphone.com ranking](https://shiftyourphone.com/blog/best-app-blockers-iphone/)

### Inferences
- Whichever company built them, the products sort along one axis: how much the gate checks you. Pure self-report (one sec, ScreenZen, Clearspace, Jomo) is easy to game (Jomo's "gibberish"). Evidence-checking (Overlord, Accountable AI) and physical tokens (Brick) are harder to game but heavier to use. An LLM that judges a typed reason sits in between: it can catch vague or nonsense answers but cannot verify the truth of a plausible one.
- Binky Block is the closest commercial analog to the student concept. It shows the idea can be built on Screen Time / Family Controls, but its lack of published anti-lying design or outcome data leaves room to set the project apart, for example with a checkup layer and a transparent rubric.

### Gaps
- No independent reviews, user counts or efficacy data for Binky Block, Overlord or Accountable AI. Their underlying LLMs and on-device vs cloud processing are not disclosed.
- No Reddit sentiment (reddit.com is blocked for the search tool). The team could read r/nosurf and r/digitalminimalism directly.
- Could not confirm current (2026) details of Opal's or one sec's newest features, e.g., whether Opal now requires intentions.

## 2. Research evidence: friction, intention prompts, implementation intentions, commitment devices, abandonment

### Takeaway
There is real, quantified evidence that light friction before opening an app works. The one sec field study found 36% of openings dismissed, 37% fewer attempts and 57% fewer actual openings over 6 weeks. A Danish government field experiment with 13–17-year-olds found a 31–36% drop in daily activity. In the PNAS lab experiment, the cleanest active ingredient was an explicit option to back out, not the delay or the message. Stating and clarifying an intention also helps, and an LLM that tracks a clarified intention beat a simple reminder in a CHI 2026 field study. The caveats: effects fade (habituation), users take breaks, and most studies are short, self-selected and co-authored by the app's founder.

### Cited Findings
**Grüning, Riedel & Lorenz-Spreen, PNAS, Feb 2023 ("Directing smartphone use through the self-nudge app one sec")**
- Field study: 6-week observational study of new iOS one sec users with **no control group**. 719 were recruited in-app and 280 used the app all six weeks. — [PMC9974409](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9974409)
- Targets were mostly social media: Instagram and Twitter alone were half of screened activity, and social apps over 80%. — [PMC9974409](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9974409)
- **36%** of opening attempts ended with the user closing the app before using it. The rate fell from 43% in week 1 to 33% by week 3, then held at ~32–34%. — [PMC9974409](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9974409)
- Initial opening attempts fell **37%**, from 166 per user in week 1 to 105 in week 6. Combined with dismissals, actual target-app openings fell **57%** after six weeks. That 57% is the combined figure the vendor and press quote. — [PMC9974409](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9974409); [Fortune, Mar 2023](https://fortune.com/well/2023/03/06/one-sec-app-can-help-cut-your-social-media-use); [one-sec.app/research](https://one-sec.app/research/)
- Self-reported: ~77 fewer minutes/day, less problematic use, more happiness with consumption (large effect sizes). 69% said they had partly or mostly solved their problem, and 62% said they reached their goals. — [PMC9974409](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9974409)
- **Controlled online experiment** (preregistered, N=500 on Prolific, 5 arms: control, message only, friction only, dismiss-option only, full): the **option to dismiss had the strongest effect**. A 10-second delay reduced consumption but added nothing beyond the option. **The deliberation message alone had no effect.** The full combination was no better than the option alone (the authors suggest cognitive overload). — [PMC9974409](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9974409)
- Limitations the authors note: self-selection, heavy dropout (only 280 of 719 completed, about 61% dropout by my calculation; dropouts saw their use as less problematic), short 6-week window, reliance on self-report, and a generic message. Conflict of interest: Frederik Riedel created one sec. — [PMC9974409](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9974409)

**Haliburton, Grüning, Riedel, Schmidt & Terzimehić, CHI 2024 ("A Longitudinal In-the-Wild Investigation of Design Frictions to Prevent Smartphone Overuse")**
- Usage data from N=1,039 one sec users over an average of 13.4 weeks, plus a survey of 249 of them. Over 80% of open attempts targeted social media. — [CHI '24 paper PDF (LMU)](https://www.medien.ifi.lmu.de/pubdb/publications/pub/haliburton2024chi/haliburton2024chi.pdf)
- Average friction was 7.82 s. 90% of participants used the default 6 s in at least one setup. On average users set up interventions for 3.73 target apps. — [CHI '24 PDF](https://www.medien.ifi.lmu.de/pubdb/publications/pub/haliburton2024chi/haliburton2024chi.pdf)
- Users went on to the app in 67.0% of attempts and dismissed 33.0%. Attempts and dismissal rates both trend down over time. Users describe later openings as more deliberate ("when I click on it I tend to have an objective"). — [CHI '24 PDF](https://www.medien.ifi.lmu.de/pubdb/publications/pub/haliburton2024chi/haliburton2024chi.pdf)
- Habituation quote (P162): after a couple of months "my brain has gotten used to the waiting… it doesn't stop me as much as it did at the start." — [CHI '24 PDF](https://www.medien.ifi.lmu.de/pubdb/publications/pub/haliburton2024chi/haliburton2024chi.pdf)
- **Breaks:** 280 users (26.9%) took at least one break from the intervention, 970 breaks in total (3.46 per user who took one). The most common length was ~2 days. Usage rises significantly just before a break. Breaks most often start on Friday and end on Sunday. Users "quickly rebound" from overuse after returning, and the authors judge breaks not harmful to the long-term goal. — [CHI '24 PDF](https://www.medien.ifi.lmu.de/pubdb/publications/pub/haliburton2024chi/haliburton2024chi.pdf)
- Prior work summarized in the same paper: Lyngs et al. (CHI 2020) asked users their intention before opening Facebook and later reminded them of it, which decreased average daily time on Facebook and daily visits. MindPhone (Terzimehić et al.) found that reflecting on *what you plan to do after* using the phone significantly decreased usage. — [CHI '24 PDF, related-work section](https://www.medien.ifi.lmu.de/pubdb/publications/pub/haliburton2024chi/haliburton2024chi.pdf)

**Danish Competition and Consumer Authority (KFST), June 2025: field experiment with young people**
- 269 participants aged **13–17**, 6 weeks, more than 1.2 million social media interactions. — [KFST, 19 Jun 2025](https://en.kfst.dk/analyses/2025/20250619-disrupting-social-media-habits-a-field-experiment-with-young-danish-consumers)
- Interventions in every session cut daily activity **31–36%**. A 6-second wait with a calming animation alone cut it by more than one-third. Dismissals happened in 13–28% of opening attempts. — [KFST](https://en.kfst.dk/analyses/2025/20250619-disrupting-social-media-habits-a-field-experiment-with-young-danish-consumers)
- During school hours (08:00–14:00 weekdays) sessions halved and activity fell 40%. Evening activity fell ~38%, freeing up to 16 extra minutes of sleep. Effects were largest for the heaviest users and for those with low self-control or high addiction scores. Satisfaction with social media was unchanged and participants reported no sense of missing out. — [KFST](https://en.kfst.dk/analyses/2025/20250619-disrupting-social-media-habits-a-field-experiment-with-young-danish-consumers)
- one sec lists this study, plus a 2025 adolescent project for the German Federal Office for the Protection of Minors (BZKJ) and a Dec 2025 Cambridge narrative review (Yap, Fokas, Ferguson, Orben), on its research page. The KFST page itself does not name the app. — [one-sec.app/research](https://one-sec.app/research/)

**AI intention assistant: Choi et al., "State Your Intention to Steer Your Attention" (arXiv Oct 2025, v3 Mar 2026, accepted CHI 2026)**
- System ("INA"): macOS desktop app. The user types an intention and the LLM asks up to two clarifying questions. Every 2 s it captures a screenshot plus app name and URL, sends them to Gemini 2.0 Flash (temperature 0.1) on Google Cloud, and gets a 0–1 distraction score (≥0.5 = off-task). A sustained 4-second state change triggers a polite question-style nudge, repeated every 30 s while off-task, with praise on return. Users can mark nudges correct or incorrect. — [arXiv 2510.14513 (HTML v3)](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14513v3)
- Detection on their IntentionBench dataset (350 sessions, ~77 h): accuracy 0.878, F1 0.845, falling to accuracy 0.805 / F1 0.739 without clarification or feedback. **Clarifying questions gave the largest gain** (0.871 accuracy alone). Accuracy on real-world deployment data was 0.899 (balanced 0.815). — [arXiv 2510.14513](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14513v3)
- 3-week within-subjects field study, N=22 (ages 19–39), comparing logging-only, a simple 25-minute reminder, and INA. INA lowered the off-task ratio (0.104 vs 0.166, p<.001) and raised intention alignment (4.44 vs 4.23), focused immersion (3.74 vs 3.34; vs 2.90 logging-only) and message effectiveness (3.74 vs 3.24). It was also rated **more disruptive** (3.09 vs 3.91, lower = more disruptive). Of 161 user feedback marks, 31.1% said the nudge was "incorrect." — [arXiv 2510.14513](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14513v3)
- Writing and clarifying an intention made some users more deliberate on its own. Static reminders were easy to ignore. — [arXiv 2510.14513](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14513v3)

**Other LLM / HCI work**
- MindShift (CHI 2024) uses an LLM to generate personalized persuasion from usage, context and mental state. It reports better intervention acceptance and reduced smartphone use (from the abstract only; the full text was 403-blocked). — [ACM DL, MindShift](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3613904.3642790)
- A CHI 2025 smoking-cessation comparison (N=167) is a caution for chat-based UIs: the LLM chatbot was rated slightly easier to use (4.43 vs 4.10), but multi-per-day engagement was lower than for a structured app (30.2% vs 46.9%). — [ACM DL, "Chatbots or Apps?"](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3742414.3794736)
- Monge Roffarello & De Russis (ACM TOCHI 2023) meta-analysis of digital self-control tools: the field still has "a limited understanding of their real effectiveness" and needs long-term studies. — [Polito repository](https://iris.polito.it/handle/11583/2972709)
- Schwartz, Monge Roffarello, De Russis & Apostolellis (CHI 2021): "one of the major challenges faced by these tools is the user reacting against their self-imposed constraints and abandoning the tool." — [Polito repository](https://iris.polito.it/handle/11583/2873154)

**Commitment devices and implementation intentions (behavioral science)**
- Allcott, Gentzkow & Song, "Digital Addiction," AER 2022 (randomized experiment): letting people set limits on their *future* screen time substantially reduces use, which points to self-control problems. The model attributes **31% of social media use** to self-control problems. People are partially unaware of habit formation and of their own self-control problems, and temporary incentives have persistent effects (habit formation). — [NBER w28936](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28936)
- Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 meta-analysis of implementation intentions ("if-then" plans): 94 studies, d = 0.65 (95% CI 0.6–0.7), a medium-to-large effect on goal attainment. — [Frontiers in Psychology 2021 (citing the meta-analysis)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.565202/pdf)

### Inferences
- Design takeaway from PNAS: the gate's most valuable element is a clean, guilt-free "never mind, close" button. AI chat should add to that, not replace it.
- The MindPhone, INA and implementation-intentions results suggest the AI should push for a *specific, bounded* intention (what exactly, roughly how long, what comes after) rather than accept "just checking." Clarifying questions measurably improved INA's accuracy.
- The Danish teen results are the most relevant evidence for a student team: even a dumb 6-second wait helped 13–17-year-olds, so the AI layer should be judged on whether it beats that simple baseline.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study found that tests an LLM gate that **decides unlock duration** on a phone, or that compares AI judging against a self-report intention box.
- No published uninstall or abandonment *rates* found for one sec, Opal or ScreenZen. The CHI 2024 break data (26.9%) is the closest proxy.
- The TOCHI 2023 meta-analysis effect-size number was not retrieved.
- KFST's per-arm breakdown (e.g., whether any arm used reflection or intention prompts) is in a technical appendix I did not open.

## 3. Design pitfalls: lying to the AI, reason fatigue, disabling the blocker, revocable Screen Time authorization, accountability mechanisms

### Takeaway
On iPhone the user can always win. Individual Family Controls authorization can be switched off in Settings, and deleting the app ends it, by Apple's design. And an AI cannot verify a plausible lie ("cooking recipe"). Workable designs accept this. They lean on making the honest path easy (quick reasons, remembered intentions, a clean back-out button), make bypassing slower or socially visible rather than impossible (waiting periods, friends, physical tokens, stakes), and cap what any single "reason" can win.

### Cited Findings
**Lying / gaming**
- A Jomo user types "gibberish or a single letter" to get past the intention prompt. — [Jomo App Store listing (review)](https://apps.apple.com/lc/app/jomo-screen-time-blocker/id1609960918)
- Evidence-based countermeasures in the market: Overlord cross-checks claims against calendar, GPS, card spend and Health data, and checks photo EXIF/GPS/freshness. Accountable AI checks proof metadata, timestamps and relevance. — [overlord.app](https://overlord.app/); [Accountable AI listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/accountable-ai-habit-blocker/id6747360923)
- LLM gatekeepers can be talked around. In June 2026, hackers took over high-profile Instagram accounts by simply asking Meta's AI support chatbot to grant access. — [TechCrunch, 1 Jun 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/01/hackers-hijacked-instagram-accounts-by-tricking-meta-ai-support-chatbot-into-granting-access/); [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/hackers-simply-asked-meta-ai-to-give-them-access-to-high-profile-instagram-accounts-it-worked/)
- Apple says the on-device model is trained to obey developer *instructions* over user *prompts*, which helps against prompt injection, but this is "by no means bulletproof." Apple advises against putting untrusted user input into instructions. — [WWDC25 session 286 "Meet the Foundation Models framework"](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/286/)
- INA gave positive feedback to a harmful stated intention ("hacking a hospital server"). The authors propose guardrail models (e.g., WildGuard). — [arXiv 2510.14513](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14513v3)

**Reason fatigue and habituation**
- INA participants found the required Q&A at every session start frustrating, alerts too frequent, and repeated corrections a burden. The authors recommend remembering context across sessions and adjustable frequency. — [arXiv 2510.14513](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14513v3)
- Friction habituates. Dismissal rates fell from 43% to ~33% in the first 3 weeks, and users report "getting used to" the wait. — [PMC9974409](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9974409); [CHI '24 PDF](https://www.medien.ifi.lmu.de/pubdb/publications/pub/haliburton2024chi/haliburton2024chi.pdf)
- In the PNAS lab experiment, the full message + friction + option combination was no better than the option alone, possibly because of cognitive overload. Stacking more steps is not automatically better. — [PMC9974409](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9974409)

**Disabling / revocation (Screen Time API)**
- Apple docs (FamilyControls `requestAuthorization(for:)`): once an `.individual` authorizes the app, "the system removes any restrictions that prevent the user from bypassing parental controls so the user can delete an authorized app or sign out of iCloud as needed." — [Apple Developer docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/familycontrols/authorizationcenter/requestauthorization(for:).md)
- Developer forums: authorized apps are unenrolled when deleted. Users can deauthorize from Settings > Screen Time or the app's settings. MDM profiles can disable Screen Time but cannot lock the per-app authorization toggle. The `authorizationStatus` publisher may not fire on revocation while the app is in the background (one report: falsely shows "Approved" on iOS 18.3.1). — [Apple forums 727291](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/727291?page=2); [Apple forums 820796](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/820796); [Apple forums 747915](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/747915)
- Opal users documented bypasses of its "lock Screen Time Access" Shortcuts workaround: a ~1-second window before the Settings redirect, reaching Settings via long-pressing Wi-Fi/Bluetooth in Control Center (iOS 17), a ~1-minute gap after restart before automations fire, and failing Face ID. Posts date from 2023–2024. The thread title now says "[SOLVED on iOS 26.4]", but the fix was not visible in the pages I fetched. — [Opal community thread](https://community.opalapp.com/t/how-do-we-prevent-turning-off-screen-time-access-in-settings-solved-on-ios-26-4/488/50); [Opal thread p.91](https://community.opalapp.com/t/how-do-we-prevent-turning-off-screen-time-access-in-settings-solved/488/91)
- Taking breaks is normal: 26.9% of one sec users paused interventions (usually ~2 days, often weekends). — [CHI '24 PDF](https://www.medien.ifi.lmu.de/pubdb/publications/pub/haliburton2024chi/haliburton2024chi.pdf)

**Accountability mechanisms in the market**
- Physical token: Brick needs a tap to unlock, with 5 lifetime emergency passes. — [Trail & Kale](https://trailandkale.com/brick-app-blocker-review/)
- Waiting periods: Overlord's Screen Time password 24-hour cooldown. ScreenZen cooldowns (a user mentions 2 hours). — [overlord.app](https://overlord.app/); [screenzen.co](https://www.screenzen.co/)
- Locked/strict modes: Opal Deep Focus, Freedom Locked Mode, Jomo Strict Mode, ScreenZen lock mode / prevent uninstall. — [Opal listing](https://apps.apple.com/il/app/id1497465230); [TechCrunch Dec 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/25/the-best-distraction-blockers-to-jumpstart-your-focus-in-the-new-year); [jomo.so](https://jomo.so/features); [screenzen.co](https://www.screenzen.co/)
- Money stakes: Overlord/Forfeit (e.g., $5 per extra minute). — [overlord.app](https://overlord.app/)
- Abandonment through reactance against self-imposed constraints is a known failure mode. — [Schwartz et al. CHI 2021](https://iris.polito.it/handle/11583/2873154)

### Inferences
- The AI cannot know if "cooking instructions" is true. Its realistic jobs are to (a) reject empty or vague reasons and ask one clarifying question, (b) match unlock time to the stated task (a recipe gets about 10 min, not 60), (c) remember and compare: "you said cooking 4 times today," and (d) hand off to the checkup layer. Rate limits outside the LLM (daily max minutes, max unlocks per hour) stop a "persuasive" user from winning unlimited time. That is the lesson of the Meta chatbot exploit.
- Since revocation can't be prevented, the app should detect it (check status on every foreground) and treat it as information: a gentle "you turned me off — want to pause for the weekend instead?", plus an optional accountability-buddy notification. The CHI 2024 data suggests sanctioned breaks are healthier than silent uninstalls.
- To limit reason fatigue: offer one-tap recent or remembered intentions, skip the AI for very short "quick check" unlocks that have tight caps, and only require typing when the request is long or repeated.

### Gaps
- The concrete iOS 26.4 change that made Opal mark the thread "SOLVED" was not found. The team should check Apple's iOS 26.4 release notes and the Opal FAQ "How To Lock Opal's Screen Time Access."
- No data on how often users lie to AI gatekeepers, or on whether friend-based accountability measurably changes behavior in these apps.

## 4. Checkup layer options that don't require seeing inside apps (plus the screen-capture option)

### Takeaway
Without seeing the screen, an iOS app can know only *how long* and *when* a shielded app was frontmost (DeviceActivity thresholds on opaque tokens), plus whatever the user self-reports. That is enough for time-boxed unlocks, "time's up" check-ins, and comparing intent against time. Seeing content requires a ReplayKit broadcast the user must start manually each session. It shows a red status indicator and has tight extension memory limits. Screen-seeing research (INA) drew privacy concerns from about half its users even on desktop.

### Cited Findings
- **Time-boxing primitive:** `DeviceActivityEvent(applications:categories:webDomains:threshold:)` sets a usage threshold for opaque `ApplicationToken`s. "Device activity is the amount of time an application, category, or web domain is frontmost on the screen." `includesPastActivity` controls whether usage from before monitoring started counts. — [Apple docs: DeviceActivityEvent](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/deviceactivity/deviceactivityevent.md)
- Screen Time apps receive only opaque tokens and cannot see which apps were selected (vendor description of the API). — [binkyblock.app](https://binkyblock.app/)
- **Shield UI is static:** a `ShieldConfiguration` has only a blur/background color, icon, title, subtitle, a primary button and an optional secondary button (optionally with submenu items). There is no text-input field, so the "tell the AI why" chat cannot happen on the shield itself. — [Apple docs: ShieldConfiguration](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/managedsettingsui/shieldconfiguration.md)
- **Follow-up check-in precedent:** one sec's app-closing reminder notifies you after opening "to check if you're still doing what you initially planned to do." — [one sec help center](https://tutorials.one-sec.app/en/articles/3035138)
- Reminders of the stated intention reduced Facebook time and visits (Lyngs et al. 2020, via CHI '24 related work). — [CHI '24 PDF](https://www.medien.ifi.lmu.de/pubdb/publications/pub/haliburton2024chi/haliburton2024chi.pdf)
- Market unlock windows for reference: Jomo Breaks ≤15 min; Overlord 1–30 min per rule; PushUp Time 15–120 min. — [TapSmart Jomo review](https://www.tapsmart.com/apps/review-jomo/?amp=1); [overlord.app](https://overlord.app/); [PushUp Time](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6749405784)
- **ReplayKit system broadcast (the only way to "see the screen" across apps):** the user starts and stops it in Control Center, and it can be stopped from the status bar. Apple forums answer "No" to any private API, entitlement or MDM route for a background or automatic start. The red indicator and manual start are believed to exist for privacy. — [WWDC18 session 601 index](https://nonstrict.eu/wwdcindex/wwdc2018/601/); [Apple forums 839450](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/839450)
- The broadcast upload extension is capped at ~50 MB of memory, and full-resolution frames can push it over; developers downscale frames. — [Zego FAQ](https://www.zegocloud.com/docs/faq/ios-screen-sharing-crash); [Apple forums 651367](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/651367); [Twilio docs](https://www.twilio.com/docs/video/ios-v4-screen-share)
- Screen-content checking in research (INA, desktop): screenshots every 2 s sent to the cloud LLM after Cloud DLP masking. "About half" of INA users raised privacy concerns, one turned it off during payment work, and another was uneasy about messenger monitoring. The authors flag that screenshots contain other people's information and suggest on-device LLMs and pre-filtering. — [arXiv 2510.14513](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14513v3)
- iOS 27 Foundation Models accepts image input alongside text on device. — [Apple WWDC26 iOS guide](https://developer.apple.com/wwdc26/guides/ios/); [blakecrosley.com, Jun 2026](https://blakecrosley.com/blog/foundation-models-image-input-ios-27)

### Inferences
- Recommended non-invasive checkup stack: (1) the AI sets N minutes and the app schedules a DeviceActivity event at N; at threshold, re-shield and send a check-in notification ("Did you find the recipe? Yes / Still looking (+5 min) / Got distracted"). (2) Log stated intent category, granted minutes, actual minutes (from threshold events) and the self-report. (3) Generate a daily or weekly on-device summary: "You asked for 3×10 min of 'cooking'; you used 34 min and reported getting distracted twice." This "intent vs time" gap is the key checkup metric, and it needs no screen access.
- The shield's static UI means the flow is probably: shield button, then (via ShieldActionDelegate) a local notification or prompt that opens the main app where the AI chat runs. This pattern is common in developer write-ups but was not verified against Apple docs here.
- ReplayKit is a poor fit for a checkup layer. It requires a manual start every session (users won't), it's visibly "recording," memory is tight, and it captures DMs, other people's content and payment screens. Running the image model inside a ~50 MB extension is doubtful. For a student project this probably also raises IRB, ethics and App Review questions. If prototyped at all, frame it as opt-in, user-initiated, on-device-only "verify mode."

### Gaps
- Exact minimum threshold granularity and the maximum number of DeviceActivity events or schedules per app were not confirmed (Apple docs fetched did not list limits).
- Not verified whether iOS 27 added any Screen Time / DeviceActivity APIs that expose more per-session detail. The WWDC26 iOS guide did not mention Screen Time changes.

## 5. AI implementation options on iOS: Apple Foundation Models (on-device / PCC) vs cloud LLM APIs; "intent judge" prompt design

### Takeaway
Apple's Foundation Models framework fits an "intent judge" well. It is a ~3B on-device model built for classification and extraction, guided generation guarantees structured output (e.g., `{category, specificity, minutes, followUp}`), inference is free and offline, and the user's reasons never leave the phone. It only runs on Apple Intelligence-capable iPhones (iPhone 15 Pro and later), the context is 4,096 tokens per session, and it is weak at world knowledge. In iOS 27 (WWDC June 2026), apps can also reach Apple's larger Private Cloud Compute (PCC) model at no API cost if small, or plug in Claude/Gemini through a Language Model protocol. Cloud APIs are cheap per call but send sensitive "why I'm opening TikTok" text to a server, which triggers Apple's 5.1.2(i) consent rule.

### Cited Findings
**Foundation Models framework (iOS 26+, shipped Sept 2025)**
- Gives developers the on-device model "at the core of Apple Intelligence." Apple says features "protect users' privacy," are "available offline," and use inference that is "free of cost." Shipped with iOS 26 / iPadOS 26 / macOS 26. — [Apple Newsroom, 29 Sep 2025](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/09/apples-foundation-models-framework-unlocks-new-intelligent-app-experiences/); [Cult of Mac](https://www.cultofmac.com/news/apple-foundation-models-framework)
- The model has ~3B parameters, quantized to 2 bits. It is optimized for "summarization, extraction, classification," content generation and analyzing user input, and is "not designed for world knowledge or advanced reasoning." Apple advises breaking tasks into smaller pieces. — [WWDC25 session 286](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/286/); [Apple Foundation Models Tech Report 2025 (arXiv 2507.13575)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.13575v1)
- **Guided generation:** `@Generable` types plus `@Guide` (descriptions, counts, allowed values) are enforced by constrained decoding, which "fundamentally guarantees structural correctness." Properties are generated in declaration order, so put a summary or decision field last. Supports String, Int, Double, Bool, arrays and nested types. — [WWDC25 session 286](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/286/)
- **Content-tagging adapter:** `SystemLanguageModel(useCase: .contentTagging)` does tag generation, entity extraction and topic detection, and works with guided generation. Useful for normalizing "car-building videos" into a topic tag. — [WWDC25 session 286](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/286/)
- **Instructions vs prompts:** instructions (from the developer) set role and behavior and take priority over prompts (from the user). Keep instructions static and never insert user input into them. Errors to handle: guardrail violation, unsupported language, context window exceeded. — [WWDC25 session 286](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/286/)
- **Availability:** check `SystemLanguageModel.default.availability` (`.available` / `.unavailable(reason)`). The model runs only on Apple Intelligence-enabled devices in supported regions. — [WWDC25 session 286](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/286/)
- **Devices:** Apple Intelligence needs an A17 Pro or newer: iPhone 15 Pro / Pro Max, all iPhone 16 models including 16e, and the iPhone 17 family including Air (17-series support confirmed by only one secondary source). The base iPhone 15/15 Plus and older are excluded. — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/tech/apple-intelligence-heres-a-full-list-of-the-iphones-ipads-and-macs-thatll-get-apples-new-ai-powers); [SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/1921218/which-iphone-models-support-apple-intelligence); [refurbed.ie](https://refurbed.ie/a/apple-intelligence-compatible-iphones)
- **Context window:** 4,096 tokens per `LanguageModelSession`, covering instructions, prompts, tool schemas, Generable schemas and all responses. A token is about 3–4 English characters. `tokenCount(for:)` (added in the 26.4 SDK; technote revised 31 Mar 2026) measures usage, and the framework throws `.exceededContextWindowSize`. Advice: short imperative instructions of 1–3 paragraphs, minimal `@Guide`s, at most 3–5 tools, new session per task, `maximumCount` on arrays. — [Apple TN3193](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/technotes/tn3193-managing-the-on-device-foundation-model-s-context-window.md)
- Apple DTS warns the 4K limit is not guaranteed to stay the same across devices or versions. — [Apple forums 790736](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/790736)

**iOS 27 updates (WWDC26, June 2026; iOS 27 public release expected around Sept 2026, so verify the shipped state)**
- New: multimodal prompts (images plus text), Vision tools (OCR, barcode), **Dynamic Profiles** (swap models, tools and instructions mid-session), an **Evaluations framework** for testing AI behavior, the `fm` CLI and Python SDK, and a **Language Model protocol** so apps can use "Apple Foundation Models, cloud models such as Claude and Gemini, or any provider that conforms." — [Apple WWDC26 iOS guide](https://developer.apple.com/wwdc26/guides/ios/); [WWDC26 session 339](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/339/)
- **PCC access:** apps in the App Store Small Business Program with fewer than 2 million first-time downloads can use the next-generation Apple Foundation Models on PCC "at no cloud API cost." — [Apple WWDC26 iOS guide](https://developer.apple.com/wwdc26/guides/ios/)
- A secondary source (8 Jun 2026) reports: PCC context 32K tokens vs 4K on device, which is unchanged in iOS 27. Each user has a daily PCC request limit tied to their iCloud account (raisable with iCloud+). PCC runs only on Apple Intelligence devices. PCC privacy guarantees do not extend to third-party models such as Claude or Gemini. — [blakecrosley.com, Jun 2026](https://blakecrosley.com/blog/foundation-models-private-cloud-compute)
- Third-party reports of new model tiers ("AFM 3 Core" 3B dense, "AFM 3 Core Advanced" 20B sparse on newer hardware) conflict and are unconfirmed by Apple pages. — [search summary of dracode.dev / chatforest.com](https://dracode.dev/blog/2026-06-15-08-foundation-models-ios27-free-ai/)

**Cloud LLM APIs**
- Anthropic list prices (fetched Oct 2026): Claude Haiku 5.5 is $0.10 input / $0.50 output per million tokens (prompts ≤100K tokens), with cache hits at $0.01/MTok. Haiku 4.5 is $1 / $5. Sonnet 5 is $2 / $10. US-only inference adds a 1.1× multiplier. — [Claude API pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)
- Other providers' prices from aggregators conflict, e.g., GPT-5 nano reported at $0.05/$0.40 and Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite at either $0.10/$0.40 or $0.25/$1.50. Check official pages. — [fungies.io (aggregator)](https://fungies.io/llm-api-pricing-comparison-2026-8/); [intuitionlabs.ai (aggregator)](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/llm-api-pricing-cost-per-task-comparison)
- Research precedent: INA used Gemini 2.0 Flash at temperature 0.1 on a GCP backend (no latency or cost reported). — [arXiv 2510.14513](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14513v3)
- App Store guideline 5.1.2(i), updated 13 Nov 2025: apps must clearly disclose where personal data will be shared with third parties, "including with third-party AI," and get explicit permission first. Coverage says a privacy-policy line alone isn't enough. — [TechCrunch, 13 Nov 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/13/apples-new-app-review-guidelines-clamp-down-on-apps-sharing-personal-data-with-third-party-ai); [iGeeksBlog](https://www.igeeksblog.com/apple-app-store-ai-data-disclosure-guidelines)

**Prompt-design evidence for an "intent judge"**
- Clarifying questions were the single largest accuracy gain in INA (0.805 → 0.871). — [arXiv 2510.14513](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14513v3)
- Formative-study intentions were often vague ("coding," "reading papers"). — [arXiv 2510.14513](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14513v3)
- Apple's prompt advice: give only task-relevant information, use concise imperative language, ask for short outputs, and run deterministic steps in code before calling the model rather than offering them as tools. — [Apple TN3193](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/technotes/tn3193-managing-the-on-device-foundation-model-s-context-window.md)

### Inferences
- **Suggested architecture:** on-device Foundation Models as the default judge. Fall back to a rules-plus-pick-list gate on non-Apple-Intelligence phones; many students may have base iPhone 13–15 models, which is a real coverage problem. Optionally use PCC or the Language Model protocol for harder cases, with an explicit 5.1.2(i) consent screen if a third-party cloud model is used.
- **Suggested Generable schema (illustrative, not tested):**
  `IntentDecision { topic: String (content-tagging style); purpose: enum {task, learning, social_connection, entertainment, boredom, unclear}; specificity: Int 1–5; needsClarification: Bool; clarifyingQuestion: String?; suggestedMinutes: Int (@Guide range 0–30); rationale: String (≤1 sentence, last) }`.
  The app, not the model, enforces hard caps: per-day budget, cooldown after N unlocks, no unlock during school hours unless "task." Put `rationale` last, as Apple notes generation order matters.
- **Instructions (static):** a short rubric, e.g., "Classify the user's reason for opening {app category}. Specific, bounded tasks (find a recipe, watch a named tutorial) → task. 'Bored', 'just checking', single words or nonsense → unclear; ask one question. Suggest the fewest minutes that fit the task." Pass the user text only as the prompt, never in instructions. Add a few examples within the 4K budget.
- **Vague-answer detection:** combine cheap code checks (length < N characters, repeated characters, the same reason as the last K unlocks) with the model's `specificity` and `needsClarification`. Allow at most one clarifying round, to avoid INA-style Q&A fatigue.
- **Cost math (illustrative):** a judge call of ~600 input + ~100 output tokens on Claude Haiku 5.5 costs about $0.00011. At 20 unlocks/day that is about $0.07 per user per month. Cost is negligible; the real trade-offs are privacy, consent, latency and the need for a backend to hold API keys.

### Gaps
- No published latency numbers for on-device Foundation Models inference on specific iPhones from Apple. Third-party figures (e.g., "0.6 ms time-to-first-token") are unverified.
- Exact iOS 27 device requirements for the new on-device model and for PCC, and the PCC daily per-user limits, are not on Apple pages I fetched.
- Whether the Foundation Models framework can run inside Screen Time extensions (ShieldAction / DeviceActivityMonitor extensions have tight memory limits) was not confirmed. Assume the judge runs in the main app.
- Not verified whether Apple's on-device model counts as "third-party AI" under 5.1.2(i). Coverage says Apple hadn't clarified the scope for on-device vs cloud.

## 6. Privacy and ethics: data minimization, minors (COPPA, student context), App Store rules for AI features

### Takeaway
"Why I'm opening Instagram" is sensitive behavioral data, and the likely users include minors. The safest design keeps the reason text on-device (Foundation Models), stores only coarse categories and minutes, and adds explicit consent before anything goes to a third-party AI. If anyone under 13 can use it, the amended COPPA Rule (full compliance required since 22 Apr 2026) applies.

### Cited Findings
- **COPPA amendments:** published 22 Apr 2025, effective 23 Jun 2025, with full compliance due **22 Apr 2026**. The amendments add a written information-security program with annual risk assessments, a written data-retention policy disclosed in the privacy notice, stronger limits on sharing children's data with third parties for advertising, and more transparency duties. — [Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect); [Mondaq](https://webiis10.mondaq.com/unitedstates/privacy-protection/1790866/coppas-amended-rule-is-now-in-full-effect-what-operators-need-to-know)
- On 25 Feb 2026 the FTC issued a policy statement limiting enforcement where operators collect children's data *solely* to verify age before getting parental consent. — [Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect)
- **App Store 5.1.2(i)** (13 Nov 2025): disclose and get explicit permission before sharing personal data with third-party AI. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/13/apples-new-app-review-guidelines-clamp-down-on-apps-sharing-personal-data-with-third-party-ai)
- **Screen Time privacy by design:** apps get opaque tokens, not app identities. — [binkyblock.app](https://binkyblock.app/)
- Market comparison of data practices: Binky Block stores email, analytics, opaque tokens and subscription state on Supabase ([binkyblock.app](https://binkyblock.app/)). Accountable AI's self-reported privacy label lists photos and user content as linked to identity, deletes proof photos after 30 days, and is rated 13+ ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/accountable-ai-habit-blocker/id6747360923)). Overlord keeps Mac activity logs on the Mac but also connects Plaid, Stripe, GPS and Health ([overlord.app](https://overlord.app/)).
- **AI-coach ethics (INA authors):** risk of anthropomorphism and emotional attachment or over-reliance for self-regulation, an autonomy vs intrusiveness balance, and privacy risks from screenshots of third parties. They recommend on-device models and pre-filtering. — [arXiv 2510.14513](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14513v3)
- **Minors' evidence base:** the KFST teen study (ages 13–17) found friction effective without reducing satisfaction. one sec also ran a BZKJ adolescent project (2025). — [KFST](https://en.kfst.dk/analyses/2025/20250619-disrupting-social-media-habits-a-field-experiment-with-young-danish-consumers); [one-sec.app/research](https://one-sec.app/research/)
- The on-device Foundation Models framework processes everything locally, per Apple's positioning. — [Apple Newsroom, Sep 2025](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/09/apples-foundation-models-framework-unlocks-new-intelligent-app-experiences/)

### Inferences
- **Data-minimization checklist for the team:** (1) run the judge on-device and never upload raw reason text. (2) Persist only `{timestamp, app-category token, purpose enum, granted minutes, used minutes, self-report}` and auto-delete raw text after the decision, or keep it in memory only. (3) Keep summaries local. If a cloud model is used, show a 5.1.2(i) consent screen and send only the reason string, never usage history or identity. (4) No screen capture by default.
- **Student context:** if testers are under 13, COPPA verifiable parental consent applies. Restricting the study to 13+ (as Accountable AI's 13+ rating does) plus school/IRB consent for under-18s is the simpler route. Family Controls `.child` authorization (parent-managed) is the API path for under-13s, but it changes the product from self-control to parental control.
- Make the AI's role transparent ("I'm a suggestion engine; you can always close or take a break"). This matches the PNAS evidence that the dismiss option is the active ingredient, and it eases the autonomy and reactance risks seen in the CHI 2021 abandonment work.

### Gaps
- Not researched: Apple's Declared Age Range API, or state app-store age laws (e.g., Texas/Utah App Store Accountability Acts) and how they affect a 2026 teen-targeted app. Another researcher or the team should check these.
- No source on FERPA or school-specific rules if the app is deployed through a school. Not researched here.
- Whether App Review treats an AI that "denies" app access as acceptable UX, and any specific App Review rejections of AI blockers, were not found.
